Continuous-Variable Kinematic Computing: A Hydrodynamic Architecture

Author: I. Anastasov  |  Research Initiative: Anastasov Theory  |  Date: June 2026

GitHub Directory: 04_RAKTS_Kinematic_Computing
Research Paper (PDF): Kinematic_Computing_Architecture.pdf

Abstract

The rapid development of modern computing has historically focused on the manipulation of discrete logic states—first through classical binary transistors (0 and 1), and more recently through the probabilistic superposition of qubits. While discrete-state quantum computing offers profound theoretical advantages, the practical engineering of these systems faces significant challenges, most notably environmental decoherence and quantum noise. This paper proposes a complementary computing architecture inspired by the Rapid Alignment Kinematic Theory of Spin (RAKTS). Rather than attempting to isolate discrete probability states from a noisy environment, this architecture embraces the environment as a continuous computational medium. By modeling information not as probabilistic amplitudes, but as continuous geometric vectors subject to physical hydrodynamic drag, we outline the theoretical foundation and Turing completeness for an Analog Kinematic Processor.

1. Vector Nodes vs. Qubits

In standard quantum computing, information is encoded in a qubit as a superposition of two orthogonal states ($|0\rangle$ and $|1\rangle$). In the RAKTS Kinematic Computing paradigm, the fundamental unit of information is the Vector Node.

  • Continuous State: A Vector Node stores data as a physical, geometric orientation (a 3D spatial vector $\vec{s}$) interacting continuously with a localized background stress-tensor field. It represents a continuous topological state rather than a discrete superposition.
  • Gradient Gating: Logic operations (gates) are not executed by multiplying unitary matrices. Instead, we induce a localized gradient in the Field Medium. The Vector Node naturally and deterministically realigns itself to minimize local field resistance, yielding the computational output without discrete wave collapse.

2. The Hydrodynamic Advantage: Noise as a Stabilizer

The primary engineering obstacle in discrete quantum computing is decoherence—the destruction of fragile qubit states by thermal and electromagnetic environmental noise, which currently requires near-absolute-zero cryogenic cooling and massive error-correction overhead.

Kinematic Computing treats interaction with the environment as the primary computational mechanism rather than a source of noise. Through the non-linear Landau-Lifshitz hydrodynamic drag, the Field Medium behaves as a viscous, energy-dissipating fluid.

When a computational impulse is applied, the medium's viscosity acts as a geometric funnel, driving the Vector Nodes into stable mathematical attractors (equilibriums). Instead of destroying the calculation, environmental interactions stabilize it, allowing for room-temperature topological processors without error-correction overhead.

3. Topological Pathfinding

Analog Kinematic Processors are uniquely suited for complex topological problems, such as route optimization and ground-state energy minimization. By structuring a computational problem as a physical network of varied flow resistances within the Field Medium, an excitation wave can traverse the entire grid simultaneously. Because the propagation naturally follows the principle of least action (Hamilton's Principle), the fluid dynamics deterministically and instantly locate the optimal path, providing a classical analog to Grover's search algorithm.

4. Turing Completeness via Background Bias (The NAND Gate)

To demonstrate universal programmability (Turing completeness), the architecture must be capable of forming a universal logic gate (such as a NAND gate) classically. In Kinematic Computing, this is achieved via Background Field Bias without transistors:

  • The Inverter (NOT Gate): The Field Medium is configured with a constant, weak background flow representing a default "True" (1) state. This background pressure acts as an inverter.
  • The NAND Gate: When two strong input vectors ($A$ and $B$) are introduced, their combined hydrodynamic drag overcomes the background bias, reversing the local flow to a "False" (0) state. This fluid-dynamic competition between input vectors and background bias produces a perfect, universal Kinematic NAND Gate.

5. Industrial Implications

  • Decoherence Immunity: Because the processor uses localized drag to reach stable attractors, environmental noise is a stabilizer rather than a disruptor.
  • Room-Temperature Operation: Bypassing the need for fragile superposition eliminates the requirement for cryogenic cooling.
  • Cryptographic Resilience: Bypasses standard integer-based decryption vulnerabilities (such as Shor's algorithm) by executing prime factorization through continuous-variable analog pathfinding.
