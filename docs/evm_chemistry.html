Abstract

For nearly a century, molecular chemistry has been bottlenecked by the computational complexity of the Schrödinger equation ($O(N^3)$ to $O(N^4)$ scaling). This "quantum bottleneck" severely limits the simulation of massive biological systems and delays pharmaceutical drug discovery. The Anastasov Emergent Valence Mechanics (EVM) engine bypasses the Schrödinger equation entirely. EVM is a high-throughput, $O(N^2)$ differentiable physics engine built as a Proof of Concept (PoC) for the Rapid Alignment Kinematic Theory of Spin (RAKTS) deterministic ontology [1]. By replacing probability wavefunctions with macroscopic fluid-dynamic vortices interacting via classical electromagnetism and the Anastasov-RAKTS Phase Exclusion ($1/r^6$) proxy, EVM accurately simulates covalent bonds, stereochemistry, and reactive dynamics. Because EVM is fully differentiable, Neural Networks can be trained directly on the engine's physical laws, enabling autonomous drug screening and material discovery. EVM demonstrates empirically that complex quantum-like structural stability emerges entirely from classical deterministic rules, establishing a rigorous paradigm shift in computational chemistry.

1. Introduction: The Quantum Bottleneck

The Deterministic Distinction: No Springs, No Rules

Modern computational chemistry relies on Density Functional Theory (DFT) or classical force fields (like LAMMPS/GROMACS). While classical Molecular Mechanics (MM) force fields scale well, they cheat by requiring arbitrary, hand-coded springs and empirical parameters for every specific bond angle. Unlike standard MM, the Anastasov EVM engine does not use arbitrary springs, nor does it enforce predefined valency rules. We do not program a Carbon atom to be a tetrahedron, and there are no "octet-rule" patches. Instead, complex stereochemistry and geometric constraints emerge autonomously from pure classical kinematics.

Quantum Mechanics has provided accurate probabilistic predictions for subatomic phenomena. The RAKTS ontology and the Anastasov EVM engine offer a deterministic mechanical framework beneath these statistics. Instead of treating the subatomic world as a probabilistic black box, EVM calculates the explicit kinematic forces turning inside.

The theoretical framework behind EVM introduces a structural departure from probabilistic orbital models: "Pauli Exclusion" and "Spin" are demonstrated to be macroscopic fluid-dynamic consequences of vortices propagating through a Field Medium. When identical vortices (electrons of the same phase) clash, they generate severe hydrodynamic shear stress, creating an impenetrable kinematic barrier (Anastasov-RAKTS Phase Exclusion). Opposite phases dynamically attract.

2. The Mathematical Framework

EVM evaluates interactions in a fully differentiable PyTorch tensor environment, operating at $O(N^2)$ complexity. The force $\vec{F}$ on any particle is the sum of three classical terms:

  1. Coulomb Electrostatics: Standard $1/r^2$ attraction/repulsion.
  2. Anastasov Steric Nuclear Shielding: An extreme short-range $1/r^{12}$ potential representing the incompressible density of the atomic nucleus.
  3. Anastasov-RAKTS Phase Exclusion & Spin-Pairing: A localized repulsive term applied only between electrons of identical phase orientation ($1/r^6$). Conversely, electrons with opposite spin phases experience a net reduction in Coulomb repulsion (the Anastasov-EVM spin_pairing scalar).

Formally, the total force $\vec{F}_i$ on any electron $i$ in the system is expressed analytically as:

$$ \begin{aligned} \vec{F}_i = &-\sum_{n}^{M} \left( k_e \frac{Z_n e^2}{r_{in}^2} \right) \hat{r}_{in} + \sum_{j \neq i}^{N} \left( k_e \frac{q_i q_j}{r_{ij}^2} (1 - S_{pair} \delta_{opposite}) \right) \hat{r}_{ij} \\ &+ \sum_{n}^{M} \left( \frac{12 C_{steric}}{r_{in}^{13}} \right) \hat{r}_{in} + \sum_{j \neq i}^{N} \delta_{\phi_i, \phi_j} \left( \frac{C_{exclusion} \cdot e^{-U_i/U_0}}{r_{ij}^6} \right) \hat{r}_{ij} \end{aligned} $$

Where $N$ is the number of electrons, $M$ is the number of nuclei, and $\delta_{\phi_i, \phi_j}$ is the Kronecker delta function for the fluid phase (spin) orientation. The exponential compressibility term $e^{-U_i/U_0}$ ensures that electrons deep in the nuclear Coulomb potential dynamically shrink their exclusion radii, separating inner-core shells from valence shells.

2.1. Computational Methodology

The EVM framework integrates equations of motion using the Anastasov-EVM Velocity Verlet Integrator with a standardized discrete timestep ($\Delta t = 0.005$ a.u.). Electron phases ($+1$ or $-1$) are statically assigned during initialization to represent stable RAKTS magnetic domains.

3. Empirical Results: The Emergence of Chemistry

To validate the deterministic ontology, the EVM engine was subjected to high-throughput structural stress tests. The results confirm that phenomena traditionally considered exclusive to Quantum Mechanics emerge autonomously from purely classical kinematics.

3.1. 3D Geometry and Orbital Hybridization (VSEPR - Methane)

In orthodox theory, Carbon forms a 3D tetrahedron (Methane, $CH_4$) due to $sp^3$ orbital hybridization. In EVM, we simulate the relaxation of Hydrogen atoms and valence electrons around a Carbon nucleus.

Result: The 10 electrons in Methane cannot form a perfectly symmetric tetrahedron unless their positive and negative spins (phases) are distributed in a specific "golden" configuration. By injecting the Anastasov optimal static phase configuration, the Anastasov-RAKTS phase exclusion autonomously forces the electrons into opposing pairs. This naturally drives the Hydrogen nuclei into a stable ~109.5° tetrahedron purely to minimize classical steric repulsion, confirming that orbital hybridization is mathematically equivalent to kinematic phase packing.

3.2. Lone Pairs and VSEPR Angles (Water & Ammonia)

In EVM simulations, non-bonding electron pairs naturally group together on one side of the Oxygen and Nitrogen nuclei, acting as localized negative charges. The resulting electrostatic and Pauli repulsion forces the O-H bonds in Water down to ~104.5°. For Ammonia, applying the Anastasov optimal static phase configuration forces the N-H bonds into a rapidly vibrating tetrahedral-like geometry reaching ~107° (minimum angle), reproducing VSEPR predictions directly from classical force balance.

3.3. Intermolecular Forces and Hydrogen Bonding (Water Dimer)

We simulated a water dimer ($(H_2O)_2$) without any explicit hydrogen-bonding terms or partial charges in the force field.

Result: The emergent polar nature of the EVM water molecules naturally organized them into a stable intermolecular network. The donor-acceptor Oxygen-Oxygen distance relaxed to ~2.78 Å, reproducing correct hydrogen-bonded geometries.

3.4. Dynamic Elasticity and Bond Dissociation (Rubber Band Test)

We subjected a Hydrogen molecule ($H_2$) to a tensile stress test.

Result: The bond exhibits a Hookean elastic regime in the range of $1.20$ to $1.55$ Å. Beyond a critical separation of $2.60$ Å, the force drops asymptotically to zero, representing a clean homolytic bond dissociation.

3.5. Steric Valency Limits (Rejection of $CH_5$)

To test octet-rule limits, we attempted to force a fifth Hydrogen atom into a fully relaxed Methane ($CH_4$) molecule.

Result: The Carbon nucleus generated a massive repulsive force. The four existing valence orbital pairs occupy the available geometric space, and their Anastasov-RAKTS Phase Exclusion shield prevents any hypervalent bonding, enforcing the octet limit algorithmically without pre-programmed rules.

3.6. Radical Recombination and Conservation (Radical Collision)

We placed two free Hydrogen radicals ($H\cdot$) at a distance of $3.0$ Å.

Result: Upon collision, the opposite-spin electrons immediately pair up via Anastasov-EVM spin_pairing attraction, forming a covalent bond. The system enters a stable, conservative vibration, demonstrating perfect conversion between potential and kinetic energy in the Anastasov-EVM Velocity Verlet Integrator without numerical energy drift.

3.7. High-Throughput Validation on the QM9 Database

To prove the engine's scalability and accuracy, EVM was tested against the QM9 molecular geometry database containing over 134,000 organic molecules. Using empirical RAKTS parameters (spin_pairing = 0.12, Exclusion: C/N/O/F=2.0, H=0.0), EVM autonomously simulated ~131,970 valid molecules in a high-throughput GPU pipeline.

The engine achieved a 99.96% structural survival rate (Root Mean Square Deviation of nuclei $\le 0.15$ Å) against ab-initio Density Functional Theory (DFT) targets, with an average RMSD of ~0.013 Å.

Without altering the universal phase exclusion constant (Excl = 2.0), EVM maintains structural stability for highly electronegative halogens (Fluorine) by mathematically accounting for the compact physical radius of the core ($R=0.14$). This confirms that complex structural stability scales robustly across hundreds of thousands of diverse molecules.

4. Industrial & Scientific Applications of EVM 4.0

This section outlines the practical and industrial applications of the EVM 4.0 engine. Driven by its unique $O(N^2)$ computational speed, explicit continuous-variable electron tracking, and full differentiability in PyTorch, EVM resolves industry bottlenecks that traditional Quantum Chemistry (DFT) and classical force fields cannot address efficiently.

  1. Ultra-fast Conformer Generation: Standard quantum methods are too slow for scanning thousands of molecular conformers, while traditional force fields ignore electron clouds. EVM relaxes thousands of candidates in parallel on consumer GPUs in seconds, acting as a high-precision structural filter.

  2. Machine Learning Data Sets: AI models (like SchNet, ANI) require millions of geometries for training. EVM generates massive, physically meaningful energy landscapes in hours instead of years, positioning it as the ultimate AI-training partner.

  3. Differentiable Drug-Target Docking: Because EVM is built natively in PyTorch, it allows for direct gradient backpropagation. Drug geometries and orientations can be optimized directly via gradient descent against protein binding pockets.

  4. Reactive Molecular Dynamics: Unlike classical MM force fields (AMBER/CHARMM) that use fixed springs, EVM bonds are not predefined. They emerge and break dynamically based on electron kinetics, enabling the simulation of combustion, polymerization, and radical biochemistry without expensive quantum overhead.

  5. Charge Transfer & Redox Simulations: With electrons modeled as explicit physical entities, EVM naturally tracks the migration of specific electrons across molecular chains, crucial for designing advanced batteries, solar panels, and organic electronics.

  6. Polymer & Materials Science: EVM simulates the physical entanglement and packing of massive polymer chains while preserving the granularity of electronic polarization, predicting material density and elasticity.

  7. Catalysis & Surface Science: Modeling metallic surfaces with quantum methods is computationally prohibitive. EVM evaluates organic molecule adsorption onto catalytic surfaces (like graphene or platinum) using direct electrostatic and steric kinetics at massive scales.

  8. Solvation Shell Dynamics: EVM natively simulates the extreme polarity of water through the explicit geometric distribution of its 10 electrons, naturally forming precise hydration shells around complex proteins and DNA.

  9. Interactive 3D Education & WebApps: Clocking in at sub-millisecond integration steps, EVM is stable and fast enough to power interactive 3D simulations where students can manually stretch chemical bonds with a mouse to observe real-time homolytic dissociation.

  10. Molecular Machines & Nano-Engines: Nanotech requires controlled rotation around bonds triggered by external electric fields or light. EVM dynamically simulates these phase shifts and electron distributions under external stimuli.

5. Discussion and Future Work

5.1. Electron Localization and Polar Centroids

In EVM, the massive classical Coulomb attraction of Heavy nuclei ($Z=6, 7, 8$) overwhelms isolated Hydrogen ($Z=1$). The valence bonding electrons are physically pulled extremely close to the heavy cores yielding an average ratio $r_{ratio} \approx 0.15 - 0.30$. While this constitutes an "extreme polarity" artifact of point-charge limitations, the overall molecular geometries remain highly stable due to compensating empirical parameters ($R_C > R_N > R_O$).

5.2. Kinematic Limitations and Future Theoretical Extensions

In the foundational EVM 4.0, the kinematic resistance of Larmor precession is simplified to static phase domains. The integration of real-time dynamic spin precession (Vector Snap) in EVM 5.0 will allow the engine to organically resolve complex transition states during reactive collisions.
