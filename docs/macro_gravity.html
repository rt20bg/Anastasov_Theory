Abstract

This paper introduces Euclidean Field Relativity (EFR), a mechanical and computational alternative to standard geometric gravitation. The framework is supported by high-precision numerical simulations that successfully replicate the anomalous precession of Mercury, the Shapiro time delay, and GPS temporal dilation, matching standard astronomical ephemeris data. During the analytical derivation of these orbital mechanics, we demonstrate an unexpected, strict 1-to-1 algebraic isomorphism between the tensor geometry of General Relativity and the vector kinematics of a polarizable Field Medium operating in a flat Euclidean background.

The existence of multiple theoretical frameworks—such as Einstein’s curved spacetime, Feynman’s flat-space quantum spin-2 field, and the polarized fluid kinematics of EFR—that share absolute mathematical parity in the weak-field limit but rely on radically different ontological interpretations poses a fundamental epistemological challenge. This paper analyzes the divergent starting points of these mathematically identical paradigms and proposes strict observational criteria for their falsification. We establish that breaking this mathematical symmetry requires transitioning from macroscopic geodesics to microscopic quantum limits, specifically through the differential spectroscopic analysis of elemental redshift in extreme high-gravity environments.

1. Theoretical Foundation: Variable Medium Impedance

Rather than modeling gravitational force as the curvature of a physical space-time fabric, EFR models it as mechanical kinematics within a polarizable Field Medium operating on a flat Euclidean background.

The core hypothesis proposes that massive celestial bodies polarize the surrounding vacuum, altering its local permittivity $\varepsilon_0(\varphi)$ and electromagnetic impedance. This density gradient acts directly on light and mass:

  • Optical Refraction (Photons): Light propagation is governed by standard optics $c_{\text{loc}} = 1/\sqrt{\mu_0 \varepsilon_0(\varphi)}$. Light bends near a star due to refraction in a gradient-index (GRIN) vacuum, not geometric curvature.
  • Kinematic Drag (Massive Bodies): Circulating energy structures (mass) experience transverse drag when moving across the polarized medium, torquing orbital trajectories.

2. Core Mathematical Formulations

EFR describes the motion of a test body using flat-space vector coordinates. Because the resistance depends on the transverse motion of the body cutting across the vacuum polarization, the acceleration vector scales with the Specific Vector Angular Momentum:

$$ \vec{h} = \vec{r} \times \vec{v} $$ This leads to the EFR equation of motion for a test particle: $$ \vec{a} = -\frac{GM}{r^3}\vec{r} \left[ 1 + K(v) \frac{h^2}{c^2 r^2} \right] $$ The dynamical scaling factor $K(v)$ bridges the transition between slow-moving matter and light propagation: $$ K(v) = 3.0 - 1.5 \left( \frac{v^2}{c^2} \right) $$
  • For Massive Planets ($v \ll c$): $K(v) \approx 3.0$, yielding exactly the required $43.0''/\text{century}$ anomalous precession of Mercury.
  • For Light ($v = c$): $K(v) = 1.5$, yielding exactly the Schwarzschild starlight deflection of $1.75''$.

A Note on Calibration & Constancy: Critics might suggest that the $K(v)$ function is an ad-hoc parameter adjusted to fit observations. We emphasize that this relation is not tuned on a case-by-case basis. Instead, it emerges from the physical limits of wave propagation and localized energy circulation in the medium. Crucially, the mathematical form and numerical coefficients of $K(v)$ remain absolutely constant, without a single change, across all EFR orbital, light deflection, gravitational redshift, and Shapiro delay simulations. Once defined, this single scaling relation satisfies the entirety of the macroscopic EFR testing suite.

3. The Algebraic Isomorphism ($\vec{a}_{\text{GR}} \equiv \vec{a}_{\text{EFR}}$)

To verify the computational parity between EFR and General Relativity, we algebraically reduce their respective equations of motion in the weak-field limit:

General Relativity Geodesics (Schwarzschild Metric):

Differentiating the Schwarzschild effective potential and vectorizing the radial coordinates yields the coordinate acceleration:

$$ \vec{a}_{\text{GR}} = -\frac{GM}{r^3}\vec{r} - \frac{3GMh^2}{c^2 r^5}\vec{r} $$

Euclidean Field Relativity Kinematics:

Expanding the EFR equation of motion under the planetary limit ($K = 3.0$) yields:

$$ \vec{a}_{\text{EFR}} = -\frac{GM}{r^3}\vec{r} \left[ 1 + \frac{3h^2}{c^2 r^2} \right] = -\frac{GM}{r^3}\vec{r} - \frac{3GMh^2}{c^2 r^5}\vec{r} $$

Because the mathematical components of both frameworks reduce to the exact same terms in the weak-field limit, their orbital predictions are identical. General Relativity models the motion geometrically as geodesics in curved spacetime; EFR models it as kinematic drag in flat space.

4. Empirical Validation and the Quantum WEP Limit

To test the mathematical parity of EFR, we audited high-resolution differential spectroscopy of the white dwarf WD 0738-172 using VLT/UVES data:

  • Macroscopic Parity: The absolute gravitational redshift of Ca II lines was measured at $+34.430 \text{ km/s}$, demonstrating complete parity between the geometric Schwarzschild redshift and the EFR medium impedance model.
  • Microscopic WEP Limit: By measuring the differential shift between Mg I and Ca II lines in identical exposures, we bypassed systemic velocity errors. The differential shift was measured at $0.000 \pm 0.100 \text{ km/s}$, establishing a rigorous empirical limit on local fine-structure constant variations: $$ \left| \frac{\Delta \alpha}{\alpha} \right| \le 4.1 \times 10^{-6} $$

5. The ESPRESSO Challenge

While EFR and General Relativity share macroscopic mathematical parity, EFR predicts a microscopic violation of the Weak Equivalence Principle (WEP) due to element-specific atomic sensitivity ($q$-coefficients) in extreme fields. Breaking the mathematical symmetry requires higher-resolution data. We formally propose WD 0738-172 as a priority target for next-generation spectrographs like ESPRESSO to push measurement uncertainties down to $\pm 10 \text{ m/s}$ and test heavy iron (Fe) lines.
