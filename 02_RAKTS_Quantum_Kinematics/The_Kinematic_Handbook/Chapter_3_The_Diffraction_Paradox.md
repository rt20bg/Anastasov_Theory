# Chapter 3: The Diffraction Paradox - The Sieve vs. The Wave

When scientists shoot electrons through a thin layer of graphite (a crystal lattice) and observe concentric glowing rings on a screen, they are witnessing the phenomenon of diffraction. 

## The Standard Story: Wave-Particle Duality
Standard quantum mechanics claims that these rings prove an electron is not a solid particle, but a "probability wave." According to this view, the electron wave hits the graphite, splits apart, passes through multiple gaps simultaneously, and interferes with *itself* before collapsing back into a point on the screen.

Because a single electron fired one-by-one produces the exact same pattern over time, the textbooks declare wave-particle duality to be an absolute, undeniable law of nature.

But is a self-interfering wave the *only* mechanical way to produce rings?

## The Entropy Paradox
Let us apply a simple stress test to the wave theory: **The Massive Beam Test.**

What happens if, instead of firing one electron at a time, we fire a massive, dense beam of a billion electrons simultaneously? 
Electrons are negatively charged; they repel each other violently. In a dense beam, there is massive Coulomb repulsion, chaotic particle collisions, and extreme entropy. 

According to the rules of quantum probability, this massive, chaotic interaction should cause immediate **decoherence**. The fragile probability waves should bump into each other, collapse early, and wash out the pattern. The screen should just show a blurry, random blob of light.

**But it doesn't.** The diffraction rings remain crystal clear, identical to the single-electron pattern.

## The RAKTS Lens: The Mechanical Sieve
How does RAKTS explain this paradox? By removing the "wave" entirely and looking at the target material.

The graphite target is not an empty space with magical properties. It is a dense, rigid, geometric cage made of carbon atoms. It has specific physical gaps, or "channels." 

Imagine throwing a handful of marbles at a heavy, rigid steel grate. 
*   Some marbles bounce back.
*   Some marbles happen to hit the gaps perfectly and fly straight through.
*   Some marbles clip the edge of the steel bars and deflect at very specific, predictable angles dictated by the geometry of the grate.

In this kinematic model, the graphite lattice acts as a **mechanical sieve**. It doesn't matter if you throw one marble or a billion marbles at once. The steel grate (the graphite lattice) is infinitely stronger than the marbles. It rigidly dictates where the marbles are physically allowed to go. The chaotic bouncing between the marbles (Coulomb entropy) is trivial compared to the absolute physical constraint of the crystal channels.

## Macroscopic Proof
If you think macroscopic physics cannot mimic quantum interference, look no further than **Yves Couder’s bouncing droplets** (Pilot-Wave Hydrodynamics). By bouncing macroscopic silicone droplets on a vibrating fluid bath, researchers have proven that a physical particle interacting with its own fluid wake perfectly reproduces double-slit interference. 

No probability. No magic. Just fluid mechanics.
