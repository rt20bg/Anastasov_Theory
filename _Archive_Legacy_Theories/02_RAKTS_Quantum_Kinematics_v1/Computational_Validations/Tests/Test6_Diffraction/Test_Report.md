# RAKTS Validation Report: Test 6 - Electron Beam Diffraction Paradox

## 1. Objective
This test challenges the core quantum mechanical assertion that electron diffraction rings (such as those seen in Teltron tubes or the Davisson-Germer experiment) are proof of "probability wavefunction collapse." The objective is to demonstrate computationally that if electrons were truly probability waves, firing a massive, high-entropy beam of mutually repelling electrons (Coulomb chaos) would destroy the interference pattern due to decoherence. The fact that the pattern remains stable proves that the concentric rings are a deterministic, kinematic result of electrons physically channeling through the rigid atomic planes of the target lattice.

## 2. Empirical Data Source
*   **Target Materials:** Polycrystalline Graphite (Carbon lattice, standard in educational Teltron tubes)
*   **Phenomena:** Debye-Scherrer concentric diffraction rings
*   **Data Type:** Spatial scattering distribution on a phosphorescent screen

## 3. Simulation Parameters (RAKTS Mechanics)
*   **Key Kinematic Variables:** Grid plane spacing ($d \approx 2.13$ Å and $1.23$ Å), impact parameter channeling, macroscopic beam Coulomb entropy (noise factor).
*   **Algorithm Approach:** Monte Carlo classical scattering simulation. Compares zero-entropy single electron scattering vs. high-entropy simultaneous beam scattering.

## 4. Results & Comparison
*   **Standard Quantum Prediction:** A single electron interferes with itself (wave-particle duality). A massive, dense beam of interacting particles should suffer massive decoherence and structural blurring due to Coulomb scattering before hitting the screen.
*   **RAKTS Kinematic Prediction:** The rigid mechanical structure of the graphite grid acts as an absolute physical steering filter. Even with massive Coulomb collisions between electrons, the exiting trajectories are tightly constrained by the physical gaps in the crystal. The rings should remain clearly visible in both single-fire and beam modes.
*   **Empirical Match:** The simulation perfectly matches the real-world operational reality of cathode-ray diffraction tubes, which produce identical crisp rings regardless of beam intensity.

## 6. Conclusion and Revisions
The kinematic model successfully replicates the diffraction rings without invoking wave-particle duality. Furthermore, the "Electron Beam Paradox" highlights a critical flaw in the probabilistic interpretation: entropy and particle collisions in dense beams do not wash out the pattern. This strongly validates the RAKTS postulate that "interference" patterns in particle physics are often macroscopic illusions generated by the rigid mechanical geometry of the target medium acting upon discrete particles.
